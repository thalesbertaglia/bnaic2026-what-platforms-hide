What Platforms Hide

How can AI researchers study recommendation, ads, tracking, and monetisation when platforms only show part of the picture?

Session idea

Platforms influence what people encounter every day: which videos are recommended, which products appear first, which ads are delivered, which posts disappear, and which creators get paid.

These are technical systems with public consequences. They shape visibility, money, moderation, and attention, but researchers usually see them from the outside: public posts, links, app traffic, metadata, and limited APIs.

This session is about what computational researchers can do in that situation. We focus on practical methods for studying platforms: auditing, measurement, NLP, multimodal analysis, network analysis, synthetic data, and research designs that work with partial access.

Participatory core

Platform Research Frontiers

Bring a method. Bring a platform problem. Leave with a sharper question than the one you walked in with.

We will use small groups to turn broad platform concerns into researchable problems: what is worth studying, which methods could help, what breaks in practice, and what could be built next.

01 What should we study?

A viral video, a buried product, a strange ad, a vanished post, a political creator making money.

02 What can each field bring?

Measurement, NLP, multimodal ML, networks, law, social theory, synthetic data, interface design.

03 Where is the frontier?

Labels, ethics, law, incentives, distribution shift, multimodal evidence.

04 What can we do next?

Audits, proxies, simulations, datasets, tools, observatories.

Expected outcome

A short, shared map of platform problems worth studying: what is technically interesting, what society needs to know, and where different fields can push the frontier.
